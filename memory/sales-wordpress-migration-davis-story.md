## The argument that works on a WordPress prospect

**Don't argue architecture. Tell the Davis story.** Russell's own client
**DavisInvestigation** outgrew WordPress fast, is now paying for a custom SQL database hosted
on **AWS** plus a **Next.js** front end (we're building it), and **nearly lost one of his
largest multimillion-dollar contracts** because of WordPress's security posture and bad
plugins.

**Why this beats a technical pitch:** the objection from these prospects is almost never
architecture, it's **control** — "we don't want to hand the web side to an agency" and "going
through you will be slow." A stack critique confirms their fear that you look down on them and
hardens them. A story about another client who *kept* full control on WordPress and paid for it
twice — rebuild plus a near-lost contract — makes the same point without condescension.

**Where WooCommerce genuinely is defensible, so don't overreach:** a company shipping a
physical product needs inventory, variants, shipping, tax and subscription billing, and Woo has
a deep ecosystem for exactly that. The real collapse comes at the layer they haven't built
yet — the **customer/operations portal**: device or case state, event history, dispatch or
audit logs, subscriber PII, compliance exposure. That is not a plugin, and bolting it onto
WordPress is where the year goes. Frame it as **exit cost arriving later**, when the business
is big enough that a rebuild is disruptive and a breach is existential.

**Russell corrected me on this (2026-07-30)** after I called Woo merely "defensible" — Davis is
him paying that bill in real time. Don't soften it again.

## Related: he won't chase small hostile contracts

Russell has clients on contracts **over $10K/month**. He walked away from Alarmion at $249/mo
year 1 rather than fight a pushy engineer for it: *"I sure as shit am not going to fight to get
a shitty little $249/mo contract not worth my time."* Small hostile accounts consume *more*
attention per dollar, not less.

**How to apply:** when advising on a prospect, weigh friction-per-dollar and opportunity cost,
not just revenue. And when declining, decline on **working-model fit**, never on the client's
size — equally honest, keeps his reputation clean, and leaves the door open for when the
portal/storefront collision arrives. See the Alarmion decline draft at
`Desktop\Alarmion-Decline-Email.md` for the wording that does this.

Buyer overlap worth remembering: alarm/monitoring, smart home and home-services companies skew
heavily to WordPress + WooCommerce, which makes them the buyer pool for both
[[product-scrollhouse-theme]] and the migration work behind it.
